Paper sizes are a quiet source of printing chaos

The world's paper does not agree with itself. A4 rules most of the planet; North America runs on Letter, slightly wider and shorter; Legal stretches long for contracts; A5 halves A4 for booklets and flyers. A PDF remembers the size it was born at, and a printer fed the wrong size must improvise — scaling unpredictably, clipping an edge, or shrinking your document into the middle of the sheet surrounded by a moat of white. PageFit removes the improvisation by rebuilding the document at the size your printer, portal or publisher actually expects.

The conversion is done properly: every original page is embedded whole and scaled proportionally onto a true target-size page, never stretched or squashed. Text stays vector-crisp, images keep their aspect, and the output is a uniform document — every page the same standard size, ready for any workflow that assumes uniformity, from duplex printing to booklet imposition to archival standards.

Proportions, anchors and the geometry of fit

Fitting one rectangle into another always involves a choice. PageFit's keep-proportions rule makes the sane one automatically: scale until the page fits entirely inside the target, so nothing is ever cropped. When aspect ratios differ — a landscape slide onto a portrait A4, say — the leftover space is distributed by the anchor you choose. Centre is the balanced default; top-left suits documents that will gain headers or be hole-punched; bottom-left suits signature-heavy pages where the foot of the page matters most.

Scaling down is lossless in quality for vector content — an A3 poster page fitted to A4 stays sharp, just smaller. Scaling up is equally honest: an A5 page enlarged to A4 keeps vector text perfect, while scanned image pages can only be as sharp as their source pixels. The mixed-size report keeps you informed throughout: it lists every distinct page size in the source — documents assembled from several origins often hide three or four — and what each becomes.

How to resize a PDF, step by step

Load the file, choose the target size, and decide orientation: 'Match each page' preserves every page's own portrait or landscape character at the new size, which is right for mixed documents; forcing portrait or landscape suits uniform deliverables. Pick an anchor if your pages are smaller than the target in one dimension, confirm keep-proportions is on (it should be, unless you have a genuinely exotic reason to distort), and press Resize.

Read the mixed-size report before downloading — it is your receipt: which sizes went in, how many pages each, and the target they became. Then download and print a single sheet to confirm the printer and the file now agree. If the document is headed for a portal with a stated size requirement, this ten-second verification is the difference between acceptance and a cryptic rejection at deadline.

The jobs that demand an exact page size

Submission systems are the strictest customers: journals, courts, exam boards and government portals routinely specify A4 or Letter and some check the actual page geometry of uploads. Print shops are the second: a booklet or flyer run begins with 'all pages A5', and a mixed-size file stops the job. International collaboration is the third — the European partner's A4 report lands in an American office whose trays hold Letter, and someone must reconcile them before the meeting handout prints.

There is also quiet standardisation work: archives that ingest decades of scans at every size imaginable, and normalise them to one for storage, viewing and OCR consistency. In all these cases the demand is the same — not approximately A4, not scaled-at-print-time, but a file whose pages are the size, uniformly. That is a small specification with an outsized effect on whether everything downstream just works.

PageFit versus print-dialog scaling

Every print dialog offers 'fit to page', so why convert the file? Because dialog scaling is temporary, per-print and per-printer: it evaporates the moment the file moves, and two printers will improvise differently. A resized PDF carries its geometry with it — the portal sees A4, the archive stores A4, every printer prints A4 without decisions. Conversion also composes: resize to A5 first and booklet imposition, n-up stacking and compression tools all receive the uniform input they work best with.

Dialog scaling has one more hidden cost — unpredictability of margins. Printers add their own unprintable edges on top of the dialog's fit calculation, occasionally clipping content that the file itself would have preserved. By fitting content inside the target page with an anchor you control, PageFit decides where the breathing room goes, once, deliberately — instead of letting three layers of software negotiate it badly at print time.

Verifying size like a print professional

After converting, confirm with your viewer's document properties: page size is stated there in millimetres or inches, and it should match your target on every page — uniformity is the deliverable, so spot-check the first, a middle and the last page. If a portal rejected your file before conversion, re-upload the converted file rather than a print-dialog rescale; validators read the file's own geometry, which is exactly what changed. Keep the pre-conversion original in the project folder: a size conversion is a one-way presentation decision, and returning to the source is how you produce the Letter edition after the A4 edition without compounding scalings. One scaling, from the master, per edition — that is the whole secret of clean size management.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will resizing stretch or distort my pages?

No. With keep-proportions on (the default), every page is scaled to fit entirely inside the target size with its aspect ratio intact. Leftover space is placed according to the anchor you choose.

What is the difference between A4 and Letter?

A4 is 595×842 pt (210×297 mm), used in most of the world. Letter is 612×792 pt (8.5×11 in), standard in North America — slightly wider and shorter. Converting avoids clipping and odd printer scaling.

Can I make landscape pages portrait?

Yes — force Portrait orientation and landscape pages are fitted onto portrait pages. 'Match each page' instead preserves each page's own orientation at the new size.

Does quality suffer when pages get bigger?

Vector text and graphics stay perfectly sharp at any size. Scanned image pages are limited by their source resolution, so a large enlargement of a low-resolution scan can look soft — the report cannot invent pixels.

My PDF has several page sizes. What happens?

That is normal for assembled documents. Every page is converted to the single target size, and the mixed-size report lists each original size and its page count for your records.

Is my file uploaded anywhere?

No. The rebuild runs entirely in your browser; your document stays on your device.
